03 · Profitability gate

The basket must outrun the click.

Most of the catalog begins below $20, while free shipping starts at $60. Bundles, subscriptions, and repeat purchase therefore belong at the center of the media strategy.

04 · Store readiness

Good plumbing. Three launch risks.

The storefront source includes a Google Ads tag, Merchant Center destination, and configured ecommerce events. The next step is verifying event firing, deduplication, order IDs, revenue, and currency.

Foundation present

Commerce events

Page view, product view, add to cart, checkout, payment, and purchase events are configured through Shopify’s Google integration. Test order and value accuracy before launch.

High priority

Health-claim policy

The catalog uses condition-led names including fertility, blood pressure, blood sugar, PCOS, thyroid, prostate, IBS, pain, and endometriosis. Google restricts products marketed as preventing, curing, or treating ailments.

High priority

Subscription block error

The rendered product HTML reports a failed Appstle subscription block. Fix and test recurring purchase before traffic arrives, because repeat purchase is central to the economics.

Medium priority

Trust gap

Full Leaf shows 639 reviews on the comparison product; Tea Relief shows 4. Traditional Medicinals prominently displays USDA Organic and B Corp proof. Surface Tea Relief’s own verifiable proof more clearly.

Medium priority

Index hygiene

A live page named “test321123” is included in the Shopify sitemap and returns 200. Remove or noindex staging pages before campaign landing-page review.

Useful asset

Founder differentiation

Daniel and Daphne’s Texas, family, fertility, and small-batch story is more defensible than generic “best wellness tea” copy—provided every supporting claim is documented.

05 · Recommended launch

A staged, evidence-led relaunch.

The first goal is to learn which product and basket can acquire a new customer profitably. Clean measurement and approved products establish the foundation for automation.

Stage 1

Clear the runway

Validate what Google will measure and what Google will approve.

  • Place test orders; confirm one purchase, one order ID, correct revenue, and correct currency in Google Ads and GA4.
  • Review Merchant Center diagnostics SKU by SKU; separate approved wellness products from condition-led products.
  • Repair the subscription widget and remove the indexable test page.
  • Pull actual AOV, contribution margin, repeat rate, refund rate, and new-customer conversion rate.
Stage 2

Launch three controlled lanes

Keep intent, economics, and policy risk separated.

  • Lane A — Calm / Sleep: exact and phrase Search to the $49.99 bundle and relevant products.
  • Lane B — Digestion / Detox: high-intent terms with dedicated product or collection landing pages.
  • Lane C — Anti-inflammatory / Turmeric: only after claim and feed review; use the founder, sourcing, and ingredient story.
  • Run brand Search separately. Use a tightly limited Shopping campaign for approved SKUs; hold condition-heavy products out.
Stage 3

Scale proven demand

Expand only from real order economics.

  • Move budget toward themes producing first-order contribution profit or a documented payback window.
  • Test $49–$69 concern-led bundles, subscription attachment, and a free-shipping basket.
  • Add Performance Max only after approved feed coverage and enough clean purchase-value data.
  • Keep fertility and disease-adjacent terms in a separate policy-reviewed expansion plan.

Method notes

  • Measured: directly observed in Google Keyword Planner, SerpApi, Google Ads Transparency Center, public product pages, sitemap, or rendered Shopify source.
  • Modelled: arithmetic based on clearly shown assumptions for decision support.
  • Assumption: an input that must be replaced by client data before action.
  • Keyword figures target the United States in English and use Google Search only.
  • Volume was deduplicated when rows shared volume and both bid bounds; volume alone was never used as a duplicate key.
  • SerpApi usage after research: 35 of 250 monthly searches. This prospect used one successful live search credit.
